# Figure 3 — caption draft

**Figure 3.** Stratigraphic framework of the Bolaven impact test. (a) Paleomagnetic sites of this study plotted at their ⁴⁰Ar/³⁹Ar ages (±2σ; Table 1) against the geomagnetic polarity time scale (GPTS 2020; Ogg, 2020). Filled and open circles are in situ flows of normal and reversed measured polarity; the record begins at WBE-A, the lowest basalt flow. Grey diamonds are clast/conglomerate sites (PKS-I, NWF-B), whose remanence does not record the field at the dated eruption. Red dashed line: the Australasian impact at ~790 ka, just before the Matuyama–Brunhes reversal. (b) Schematic composite stratigraphy of the impact target and its cover (thicknesses not to scale): Khorat Group fluvial–floodplain mudstone/siltstone and cross-bedded quartz sandstone, an erosional unconformity, and the Bolaven basalt cap (≤300 m; ~16 Ma–27 ka) weathered at the top to lateritic/bauxitic saprolite. The ~790 ka impact level separates pre-impact flows (PKS-B and the basal flow WBE-A) from post-impact flows (NWF-A, PKS-J, PKS-K) that bury the crater; off the plateau the ejecta breccia rests on the pre-impact surface. (c) Composite sketch of the proximal-to-distal ejecta breccia and overlying sandy silt (vertical extent ~0.5–10 m), redrawn and modified after Sieh et al. (2023). The breccia rests on pre-impact substrate (Mesozoic sandstone and mudstone, weathered basalt flows, fluvial gravels), fines upward and with distance from the proposed crater, carries unweathered basalt and sandstone boulders near its top proximally and vein-quartz clasts near its base distally, and has tektites at and just below its upper contact beneath a pebble lag and the sandy silt; no single exposure shows all of these features. (d–h) Field photographs from the March 2024 sampling campaign: (d) WBE-A, the basal in situ flow (person for scale); (e) NWF-A, an in situ flow exposed in a stream bed (notebook for scale); (f) NWF-C, basalt boulders in the red diamicton; (g) an NWF-C boulder with the orientation plate in place before sampling; (h) PKS-G, basalt boulders on a coffee-farm hillslope.
