# Figure 5 — Tektite major-element geochemistry and rj-MCMC source unmixing

(rebuilt 2026-11-13 without the felsic metatuff; numbers match Code/geochem_rjmcmc/figures/summary.json)

**Figure 5.** Major-element composition of Laos and Thailand tektites and reversible-jump MCMC unmixing of their source (after Carter et al. 2025). (a) EPMA analytical totals per tektite; analyses below 97 wt% (open grey) are rejected, leaving 82 accepted analyses on four tektites. (b–h) Anhydrous Harker diagrams of the accepted analyses (MgO, CaO, Na₂O and K₂O on log scales, to show how strongly the regional sediments and regolith are leached), compared with published Indochinite and Muong Nong tektites (grey; n = 262) and with the median compositions of the candidate source lithologies (stars): this study and the compilation of Sieh et al. (2020), with the Bolaven basalts divided into tholeiite and alkali basalt, and unweathered post-Archean shale (PAAS; Taylor and McLennan 1985). (i) Log-likelihood and (j) number of components K for the 12 chains of the full model (6 × 10⁴ iterations; dotted line = end of burn-in); all chains fit equally well with very different K. (k) Posterior mixing weights by lithology type (mean ± 1σ, combining within- and between-chain variance; dots are chain means). (l) Posterior-predictive residuals (model − observed, % of observed) for each oxide. (m) Mean mixing weights in the full model and the three sensitivity runs: no random component; Na₂O and K₂O excluded; and regional lithologies only (no PAAS or random component). The weights of all runs are listed in Table S5.
