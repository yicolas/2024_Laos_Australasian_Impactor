# Figure 4 — caption draft (replaces LEAD_3 Figs 4–8)

**Figure 4.** Rock magnetism of Bolaven basalts. (a, b) Hysteresis loops (high-field paramagnetic slope removed, normalised to Ms) of paired fresh-interior (blue) and weathered-rind (red) chips from the ejecta clast NWF-C and the in situ flow PKS-B (1155 ka). Loops close by ~300 mT and are narrow and steep through the origin, indicating a low-coercivity, magnetite-family assemblage with no hematite-like contribution; the weathered rinds have lower squareness. (c) Day plot (Day et al., 1977; Dunlop, 2002) of seven fresh (filled) → weathered (open) pairs from in situ flows (circles), the PKS-I corestone (diamond) and diamicton clasts (triangles). All specimens lie in the pseudo-single-domain field, and every pair shifts in the same sense: weathering lowers Mr/Ms (mean 0.29 fresh vs 0.23 weathered) toward more multidomain-like behaviour, independent of site type or eruption age. (d) Low-temperature (MPMS) remanence of NWF-C: zero-field-cooled and field-cooled low-temperature SIRM on warming (blue) and room-temperature SIRM on cooling and warming (orange), each normalised to its maximum. No Verwey transition is resolved near 120 K (shaded), consistent with oxidation of primary magnetite toward maghemite; the steep rise below ~30 K reflects ultrafine, near-superparamagnetic grains. (e–j) FORC diagrams (local-polynomial fit, smoothing factor 5; 10 for the finer-step PKS-B measurement) of fresh interiors (e–g) and weathered rinds (h–j). All show a central ridge along Bu = 0 with limited vertical spread (weakly interacting PSD/SD-like magnetite) and no high-coercivity or strongly interacting component. Ridge coercivities (≈10–17 mT) match bulk Hc; the fresh PKS-I interior is the exception, with a broad, low-coercivity distribution peaking at ≤5 mT (MD-like) compared with ~15 mT in its weathered rind. White strip at low Bc: region excluded where the smoothing window extends beyond the measured data. (k) Back-scattered-electron image of fresh basalt LS-H18 (Area 1) with the ten EDS spots; orange rings mark the titanomagnetite grains (the pale grain at upper right hosts the secondary Fe–Mn oxide). (l) EDS compositions of all ten spots (wt%, normalised to 100 %, all Fe as FeO). The four titanomagnetite grains (one Cr-rich) consist of 79–96 wt% FeO + TiO₂ and are the only Fe–Ti oxides, identifying titanomagnetite as the primary remanence carrier; the silicates (olivine Fo53–78, augite, labradorite An59–62) and a secondary Fe–Mn oxide make up the rest of the assemblage. No shock, high-pressure or metallic phases were found.
